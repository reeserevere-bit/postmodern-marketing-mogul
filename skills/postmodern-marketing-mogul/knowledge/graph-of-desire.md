                                 SUBJECT, SELF & SIGNIFIERS
You are born into the world as a Black American (Triangle), forced to interact with the world through the

English language (AAVE)*, what Lacan calls Signifiers (S).



Through language you learn to differentiate between categories of meaning; what we call the Signified

(S’), or easier to understand as the vast database of all words and how they describe each other.



The learning of AAVE and all its nuance is what stamps the Postmodern

Negro permanently as an originator of desire.
This is why we call him the split subject $. Every word he learns, he learns in comparison to every

other word, its value against all the rest. Only in this way can he become aware of everything

possible, every object, every manner of being, and exactly where it ranks in society. But knowing the

ranking forces something else on him, he must now ask; where he will fit in it?



His answer to this question is the precondition for the development of a self

worth, and it's also where he splits.


Without a answer there is no self to communicate. With it, he must picture every self he could possibly

become, pick one as his ideal, build a temporary version of himself to face society while he isn't there yet,

and communicate that self to others, all using a system of language and values he must rent from society.

This gap is where desire is born, and never resolves. A perpetual tax debted to

the Postmodern Negro living in capitalist society.




       Consider: a slider, a putt-putt, a hoopty, a whip, wheels, a donk, a vert, a old school, scat, lamb chop. All of these allude to the same thing, an automobile. Once the
       Postmodern Negro learns the difference between these words and understands the (current) ranking between them, he automatically ranks himself amongst them.
       No longer "I need an automobile" but rather "I see society has a ladder of cars, and I DESIRE the top one."


         *AAVE- African American Vernacular English

                                      Upper & Lower Self
You are now a split subject forced to exist in two psychological loops at once:                                                  UPPER
The upper is extroverted, it's how the Postmodern Negro speaks to
and deals with society.
The lower is introverted, it's how he consults himself before he ever
opens his mouth, his inner monologue.

The upper loop: the signifier enters his mind and moves through s(O), signified of the other, then
reaches O, society itself, who tells him exactly how those words differ from each other in this
moment in history (late 2020s). With that understanding he goes back to s(O), then is able to
communicate to others with his Voice. This entire process happens before he speaks. He can never
create new meaning, only rent it from society (the other).

This is where slang is born; being fly, having that on, swag, being
fresh, coming trim, all several variations of the same ideal; put
together yet the one we use is the one [negro] society currently
deems “in fashion”

The lower loop is how the Negro consults himself. The path starts from split subject to i(o), the
ideal ego, who he wants to be, the things he aspires to as a person, then to E, his ego, who he is                              LOWER
right now, then lastly to I(O), the ego ideal; who Negro society wants a Negro to be. This loop is
where his self esteem is formed, and is constantly running underneath the entire loop before he
ever speaks.


These two circuits never line up.

It's why what a negro says and who he is rarely match, because one loop
is how he faces the world and the other is how he faces himself.
There is always a gap, and that gap is where desire grows like a bacteria or a fungus. The reason
he keeps desiring is because he's trying to lay carpet in a black hole (split psyche).

          Social Feedback & Identity Construction
 Graph I maps your internal thoughts.

 Graph II maps your external voice.

 Graph III is a bit more metaphysical; it maps your exact relationship with
 reality and consumerism.

 Here is the flow:

 The Postmodern Negro (the split subject, $) wakes up, grabs his ideal ego i(o), and
 walks out the door into society (the Other). Moving through society, he grabs desire (d)
 and with it he forms a relationship with object petit a; the object he believes will
 complete him, but never does because he ends up wanting something else.

 The next morning, as he walks through society, he is stopped. Society taps him on the
 shoulder and asks: Che vuoi?

 Direct translation from Lacan this means simply "What do you want?". For our
 purposes ,we can understand this to mean:
 “In a hyper-capitalist America, What do YOU want,
 Black man?"
 Because you don't have a clear answer to this question, you are forced to develop a
 Fantasy.

 Look at the exact symbol for the fantasy: ($ a)◊
                                                                                 ◊
 It is made of two parts. It is you, the split subject ($) permanently linked ( ) to the
 unachievable object (a). Your entire fantasy is intertwined with you being tied to an
 object you can never actually have.*

 This is the exact psychological framework behind Postmodern Negro fads like
 reposting aspirational quotes to your story or creating a vision board,
 manifestation. You are forced to create a visual reality, linking yourself to the
 objects you desire, to shield yourself from the fact that society has no real
 answer and you are looking into a void.

 The vision board is a defense mechanism against the loneliness and vastness of the
 void. The negro curates these images to find comfort in the emptiness.

                         GRATIFICATION & REPRESSION
The graph is now complete.


The top loop is your subconscious, what the Postmodern Negro may not have the words to explain, running on top of the
bottom loop, which is all that he can muster to communicate from the bounds of his rationality.


The Top Loop starts with jouissance. Lacan defines jouissance as pleasure pushed past the point of comfort, maybe like the
feeling of being tickled? Pleasure to the point of discomfort he says.


TO BE HONEST I DON'T KNOW WHAT THE FUCK HE MEANS BY THAT.

How I understand it, applied to the Negro, this term simply means instant gratification. I wanted it, I bought it, you can't tell
me shit 'cause I get what I want, it's my world; and " My God don't play bout meeeeee."


From jouissance we go to S(Ⱥ), the signifier of the lack. Essentially the hole in society, proof that society itself doesn't have
the final answer either. Because society can't give you the answer, your instinct is to demand it anyway. This pushes you
forward into ($   ◊ D) The Drive; or in our words, The Hustle. Here the negro demands the broken system gives him what he
wants by exerting physical energy for it chaining himself to an endless grind earn capital for the use of capital expenditure.
But Demand always outruns satisfaction. So from The Hustle, we exit straight into castration which Lacan defines it as a
deep, structural sense of incompleteness, accepting that neither you nor the Other actually has "it," whatever "it" is.


But to be fair with the reader, I DON'T KNOW WHAT THE FUCK HE MEANS BY
THAT EITHER.
