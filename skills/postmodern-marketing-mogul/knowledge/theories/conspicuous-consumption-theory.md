Conspicuous consumption theory, originally coined by Thorstein Veblen, revolves around the idea that people make purchases not merely for utility but to signal their status, wealth, values, or identity to others. Conscious consumption and conspicuous consumption are intertwined but distinct. Conscious consumption focuses on deliberate purchasing decisions that reflect ethical, environmental, or social values (e.g., buying sustainable or fair-trade products). Conspicuous consumption, on the other hand, is about outwardly signaling status, identity, or values to others. Today, they overlap significantly because people often conspicuously consume _conscious products_ to signal their moral or social alignment.

**Relevance in 2025 and beyond:**

1. **Social Media as a Catalyst:**
    
    - Social media amplifies conspicuous consumption by providing a constant, public stage. Platforms like Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube enable individuals to showcase their lifestyles, purchases, and experiences to a global audience.
    - Algorithms prioritize aspirational content, making conspicuous consumption a normalized, even expected, behavior for social validation and influence.
2. **Technology’s Role in Medium and Metrics:**
    
    - Technologies like augmented reality, virtual reality, and NFTs are creating digital spaces where conspicuous consumption can occur beyond physical goods (e.g., virtual fashion or collectibles displayed in online environments).
    - Metrics like likes, shares, and comments quantify the social capital gained from conspicuous consumption, driving further participation.
3. **Future Trends:**
    
    - **Hyper-personalization:** As AI and data analytics advance, products and advertisements will target individuals more precisely, enabling conspicuous consumption tailored to specific social groups or niches.
    - **Virtual economies:** The rise of the metaverse and digital goods will transform how status is signaled, with ownership of rare or exclusive digital assets becoming new symbols of wealth or influence.
    - **Ethical signaling:** Social and environmental concerns will increasingly drive conspicuous consumption as people seek to showcase their values as part of their personal brand.
