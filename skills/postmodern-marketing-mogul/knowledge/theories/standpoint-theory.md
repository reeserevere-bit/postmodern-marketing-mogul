# standpoint theory

**Standpoint theory** is a concept in social theory that emphasizes the importance of **perspective** in shaping knowledge and understanding. It suggests that people’s **social positions**, such as their gender, race, class, and culture, influence how they see the world and what they know about it. The theory argues that those who are **marginalized** or **oppressed** in society have unique insights and perspectives that are often overlooked or dismissed by those in power.

In **postmodernism**, standpoint theory connects by challenging the idea that knowledge can be objective and universal. Postmodernism is skeptical of **grand narratives** or **universal truths**, and standpoint theory fits into this by arguing that knowledge is always situated within specific contexts. No single perspective is more valid than another, and all viewpoints are shaped by the social and historical positions of the people holding them.

In this sense, standpoint theory aligns with **postmodernism**'s emphasis on **subjectivity**, the idea that all knowledge is shaped by the perspectives and biases of those who create it. By prioritizing the experiences and perspectives of marginalized groups, standpoint theory critiques the power dynamics that shape traditional knowledge, aligning with postmodern critiques of objectivity and authority.
