# Uses and Gratification theory
https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Carolyn-Lin/publication/325673264_Uses_and_GratificationsAudience_uses_and_gratifications_for_mass_media_A_theoretical_perspective/links/5b1cc2f145851587f29e5dd8/Uses-and-GratificationsAudience-uses-and-gratifications-for-mass-media-A-theoretical-perspective.pdf

Examines how consumers actively engage with media, including advertising, to satisfy specific needs such as social connection, information, or entertainment.

viewing audiences as active consumers who select media based off what they want to gain from it (why people choose certain media and what the seek to gain from that choice)

differs from other theories by assuming audiences are not passive recipients of media that hey choose what they consume to meet their needs

theory seeks to explain 1) the psychological needs that help shape 2) why people use the media and that motivate people to 3) engage in media use behaviours to 4) derive gratifications to 5) fulfill those intrinsic needs within the confines of a specific sociocultural enviroment. 

says individuals exposed to the same content will respond to it in different ways depending on their characterisitcs

	Example applications:
- **Social media usage:**
    Someone might use Facebook to stay connected with friends and family, fulfilling their social needs. 
- **News consumption:**
    A person might watch a specific news channel to get informed about current events, satisfying their cognitive need for information. 
- **Entertainment media:**
    Watching a comedy show could be used to relax and escape from daily stress, fulfilling an escapist need. 
Criticisms of the theory:
- **Self-reported data:**
    The theory often relies on self-reported data, which can be subjective and may not accurately reflect people's true motivations for media consumption. 
- **Overemphasis on individual choice:**
    Critics argue that the theory may not adequately consider the influence of social and cultural factors on media selection.

**IN MARKETING*
*
 ==marketers should understand what motivates customers to engage with their brand by identifying the psychological and social gratifications they seek from consuming their products or content, rather than just passively receiving messages; essentially, it focuses on understanding why people choose certain brands based on what needs those brands satisfy for them.==

- **Application in marketing strategies:**
    
    - **Target audience segmentation:** Identify different consumer segments based on their needs and motivations. 
    - **Content creation:** Develop content that directly addresses the specific gratifications sought by the target audience. 
    - **Channel selection:** Choose the right media platform to reach consumers based on their preferred method of accessing information and entertainment. 
    - **Brand positioning:** Position the brand as a solution to the consumer's specific needs and desires. 
    

Example of using uses and gratifications theory:

- **A fitness brand creating content that emphasizes social connection and community building to appeal to people seeking social interaction through exercise** .
- **A news website tailoring its articles to provide in-depth analysis to satisfy users seeking to gain a deeper understanding of current events** .

DEEPER
	two basic psychological needs are deficiency and non deficiency needs. 
	deficiency needs come from internal dissatisfaction like love and security and rely mostly on other people for fulfilment
	non deficiency needs are self actualisation needs that can be fulfilled independently and enhance self development
5 different self actualization needs relevant to media are 
	1. cognitive needs- like the need to understand
	2. affective needs- needs that strengthen aesthetic or emotional experience
	3. integrative needs- that strengthen ones confidence, credibility, or stability
	4. needs related to strengthening contact with family friends and the world
	5. needs related to escape or tension release

ritualistic orientation- describes audiences who consume media out of habit and have less defined gratification goals. tend to be more motivated by diversion 
instrumental orientation- portrays audiences whose media consumption is more intentional

expectancy value theory-rooted in social psychology. an individuals behaviorial intentions are guided by the likelihood and value of its consequences.
