# Agenda Setting Theory
Argues that media, including advertising, ==doesn’t tell people what to think but what to think about==. It frames products or services as central to current trends or societal issues.

 introduced by Maxwell McCombs and Donald Shaw in 1972
media shapes public perception by determining which topics are important

"front page news or extensive airtime signals to the audience that these issues are significant"

repeated exposure makes it more prominent in audiences minds
first level
	focuses on the salience of issues. media highlight certain topics and influences what the public sees as important
second level
	examines how media frames these issues by emphasizing certain specific attributes or perspectives shaping how audiences think about them

gatekeepers - editors and journalists decide which stories are covered and how they are presented

Types of Agenda Setting:
	•	Media Agenda: The priorities set by media outlets.
	•	Public Agenda: Issues perceived as important by the public.
	•	Policy Agenda: Topics that influence policymaking, often shaped by both media and public agendas

Example: Marketing a Product Using Agenda-Setting
Imagine you are launching a health drink:
	3.	Media Agenda: Partner with health-focused media outlets and influencers to promote the drink as a must-have for fitness enthusiasts.
	2.	Public Agenda: Highlight how the drink addresses common health concerns like energy levels or hydration through targeted content.
	3.	Framing: Emphasize its natural ingredients and benefits through storytelling,e.g., testimonials from athletes or nutritionists.
	4.	Crisis Management: If there are concerns about sugar content, shift focus to its other benefits, like vitamins or antioxidants.
By strategically influencing what consumers think about (first level) and how they think about it (second level), you can effectively position your product in the market and build a strong brand narrative
