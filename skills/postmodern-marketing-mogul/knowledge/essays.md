### 1. Jean Baudrillard’s Hyperreality (Clarified)
Concept : Jean Baudrillard’s theory of hyperreality explains a world where the distinction between reality and simulation collapses. In hyperreality, simulations (images, performances, symbols) no longer reflect reality; they replace it. Indeed becoming reality itself. The simulation becomes the “truth,” even if no original ever existed. Meaning at this stage is no longer drawn from what is real , but from what is seen and believed .
Real-World Example :
Let’s take a real-world example from my own dating life during this period, Sending your significant other a simple “Happy Birthday” text on their birthday is real affection. It is their birthday, you remembered and you very well text them for any other miniscule matter. A simple text communicates to them you remembered their birthday and are happy for them.
That won’t cut it in 2025.
One is forced (silently obligated) to bring the celebration of their partner’s birth into the hyperrality playground we call social media.
Posting a filtered, curated Instagram collage of pictures of the both of you on your story, and tagging her with “Happy Birthday Babe, I love you forever ❤️👑” is hyperreal affection . It’s not for her- the text was just for her. The post is for the social simulation of love. The projection to your followers and hers the status and conviction of your relationship.
A crucial consideration: If you don’t post her publicly, even if you text her privately and spend time together, your partner may begin to feel unloved . Not because you failed to love her, but because you failed to perform love within the digital simulation where validation and attention now reside. This is a clear example where the simulation has murdered the real.
The consequence is delayed, subtle and not often visible but very much real. Over time, you lose relational capital with your partner not because of absence of feeling, but because of failure to participate in the symbolic theater where that feeling is now measured.
This is the core of hyperreality in 2025:

The Postmodern Negro finds himself in a rather pecuilur postition in the land from which he currently breathes and pays taxes to. Piggybacking on US Hegemony in the world as the dominant global culture the postmodern Negro takes a deep pride in being the subject of influence in the areas of fashion, music, and dance across the globe, yet this influence has done very little to increase the position of the Postmodern Negro in American society. Given the hastiness with which postmodernism infects everyday life in the 2020s, one must consider the enviroment devloping the mental scaffoliding of the Negro coming to age in such conditions. By postmodern, I refer to a society characterized by the collapse of grand narratives, the dominance of simulation over reality, and the construction of identity through consumption rather than traditional cultural markers. Where utility is secondary to an object’s social purpose (a purse for the holding of misc objects vs a Hermes Birkin Bag). A point in history where all hierarchy of needs are met to the point of not needing to stress over where their next meal should come but rather, ample time is spent in reflection of what instagram aesthetic they should curate next, promptly followed by the deep consideration of “Which brands in the vacuum of all brands available for purchase will increase my social standing in a society, where we measure wealth through its material display?” Something of a nostalgic yearning for the tangibility of wealth seems to press upon the Postmodern Negro psyche, which should come to no suprise, as this Negro is the first born into a consumer society where his every desire can be satisfied instantaneously, hardly what his ancestors experienced decades ago. Take the Postmodern American society at large; who moralize through consumption (buy black, support small business, boycott) and consider the place in said society for the Negro; how does he operate in such conditions? Further questions for the readers reflection through these collection of essays:
- How does the Black American find truth in a post-truth society?

The answers to these questions are quite verbose. Before we begin to reason our way into a “answer” I believe that we must segment the negro population, as they are not all a monolith, one cohesive culture, but perhaps several clans who share the same skin color. A simple enough segment for our purposes will be that of the black lower class , black middle class, and black upper class. We must note here these are only base economic dividers as black culture could be splintered into black religious groups, black skater groups, black sports fans, black intellectuals, Etc.. this presents a potentially rewarding revelation for a future Negro sociologist with a deep attention to detail. However, for now we have a more compelling piece of sociology to uncover; From a first hand account I can tell you that the Post modern lower class black american has no competition in the world as it relates to conspicuous consumption. Perhaps one of the few arenas outside of sports and entertainment where the Negro is set free to display the prowess of his purchasing power.
To understand the lower class negro you must firstly understand 3 things in regards to this segment: This lower class Postmodern Negro has been firstly born 500 years into a capitalist society where surplus is the norm, Secondly prone to large displays of wealth as a means to differentiate themselves in a environment banded by its lack of resources, and lastly a participant in popular consumer culture- staying abreast with all the latest trends and carrying a mind full of arbitrary brand hierarchies (Polo is better than polo assn, tommy hilifiguer is better than nike, Gucci better than all). When you combine these 3 elements in a budding young negro; walla you have just crafted what we can henceforth reference as Capitalisms Champion Consumer (CCC). . A consumer so blinded by the world of symbols he willingly works (in some cases multiple jobs) to purchase brands for the means of flaunting, as in finding an identity within the consumption, from here we have set the foundation to find the answer to the previous questions.
I contrast this from the Postmodern Negro Lower class to the PostModern Negro middle class, from which being born to two teachers I am indisputably a product of. In terms of conspicuous consumption, things seem to slow in this bracket. A first hand account where I can tell you as a Postmodern Negro adelescent in middle school (during 2012-2015) your entire social standing depending purely in relation to the shoes in which you could acquire (since we all had to wear uniform, please note in schools without uniform the effects may be exaggerated) I noticed very quickly that those of my peers who could care less what was happening in class instead placing a higher emphasis on the brands in which they had on in comparison to others & their social capital rewarded from such purchases were those from the lower class. So, the position of the black middle class Postmodern is quite interesting, one of light envy towards the lower class Negro as the dominant force in “black culture” setting the overall societal norms for the race. This is of key concern, however the entire mentality is not built from envy, however in the middle class segment specifically, considferable attention must be given to what “type” of negro one may be. Common Lines of thinking may be “I am obviously not a lower income black - i dont live in the projects, I dont speak in eubonics, and I am not considered by my peers as “fly” so what does it mean to be me in a society where people are fairly more acquainted with the lower class?” I can also contrast the values of the middle class. Whereas my family was far less concerned of the latest trending brands and viewed the prices of certain items as exorbitant and ridiculous “for a pair of shoes” i was clothed rather averagely, and as you can imagine, average in a social setting run by sign hierarchy was basically suicide. In all honesty with my reader one must recollect the feelings of ignominy experienced when arriving to school and realising over the weekend it was a new jordan drop (i was out of touch with the drops as there was no need to follow as my parents obviously wouldnt purchase) and to see almost the whole school have them and feel left out. This is what I would argue is a key tenet of post modern black middle class life: envy of the consumption patterns expressed by the lower class during the time period in which your sense of value, identity, and understanding of the world is cementing. Options available to the middle class postmodern are bifurcated, either:
A: further perpetratte and fully immerse the spending habits of the lower class and gain the social capital rewarded for such actions
B: remain indifferent, live a life decentered from brands and perpetuate middle class values at the risk of a glass ceiling placed on ur social mobility
Every middle class post modern negro you meet in the wild will be at some point in their journey between those two points. In earlier vernacular a negro with too many “white” qualities may have been referred to as a Buppie(a young black proffessional person)- Today I can say with authority that the modern equivalent would be similar to that of a “oreo” something reserved for those who are “white on the inside but black on the outside” very prevalent slur (if you will) to hear in middle class suburbia where the negro adolecsnet is far removed from the prevaling dominant lower class negro cultutre but still must fit into the example they have set forth.

The themes of these shows tend to revolve around violence, and debauchery but one must note in the media reflected to the postmodern Negro these themes are flipped on their head and interpreted as coming to power, or ego assertion in a world where the Negro is constantly reminded of his previous position of subservience, perhaps one could understand why these themes would strike something latent in the subconscious of the Negro. So now we understand the overarching current of society we must refer to our right hand and ask how does this apply to the negro? A very fitting term has arisen to describe this “conspicuous consumption” meaning the purchasing of items for the flaunting infront of others.
